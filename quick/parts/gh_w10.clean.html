(c) Louvain. It directly optimises modularity to find community structure. PageRank ranks node importance, K-Means clusters vectors, and ARIMA is a time-series method.

(b). HIPAA = Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act. It covers protected health information. Financial data falls under GLBA.

Pool · T/FQ10.5 Google Flu Trends successfully replaced traditional CDC flu surveillance and has been used as the primary source ever since.

False. GFT over-predicted flu by about 2× in 2012–13 because it over-fit to search terms. Google discontinued it. Search data can only supplement traditional surveillance.

Pool · T/FQ10.6 Time series data is order-sensitive: randomly shuffling the records in a time series changes the analysis result.

True. Trend, seasonality and autocorrelation depend on temporal order. Shuffling destroys them.

Pool · T/FQ10.7 Social network graphs are typically sparse, with most nodes connected to only a small fraction of all possible neighbours.

True. Real networks are scale-free (power-law degree). Most nodes have few links, and a few hubs have millions. So edges ≪ n2.

Pool · T/FQ10.8 Medical imaging data such as MRI and CT scans is classified as structured data because it is stored in files.

False. MRI/CT images are unstructured binary files (DICOM) with no row-column schema. They need computer-vision models, not SQL.

Pool · DescriptiveQ10.9 D1. Describe three types of analysis commonly performed on social media data (e.g., community detection, sentiment analysis, influence maximisation). For each, state one specific ethical risk that arises when it is deployed at scale.
  1. Community detection (Louvain, modularity) groups densely connected users. Risk: inferring sensitive membership (political, religious, sexual orientation), then profiling or targeting groups.
  2. Sentiment analysis (VADER, transformers) scores opinion polarity. Risk: biased classifiers that mis-score dialects and Bangla-English code-mixed text. Others may use it to watch for dissent.
  3. Influence maximisation (independent cascade, greedy/CELF) picks seed users to maximise spread. Risk: manipulation and misinformation campaigns at scale, and using people without their consent.
Pool · DescriptiveQ10.10 D2. What is the difference between ARIMA and Facebook Prophet for time series forecasting? When would you prefer Prophet over ARIMA? When might ARIMA still be the better choice?

ARIMA(p,d,q) is a statistical model. It combines autoregression on past values, differencing for stationarity, and moving-average errors. It needs regular spacing, stationarity checks and manual order selection.

Prophet is an additive curve-fitting model: y = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + ε (piecewise trend with changepoints, Fourier seasonality, holiday effects). It tolerates missing data and outliers. It is interpretable and easy to run for thousands of series.

Prefer Prophet for multiple strong seasonalities, holidays (Eid, Black Friday), trend changes, missing data, or many series forecast by non-experts. Prefer ARIMA for short-horizon forecasts of stationary series with strong autocorrelation and little seasonality, for small data, or when you need a rigorous statistical model with a well-defined error structure.

Pool · DescriptiveQ10.11 D3. Identify three data integration and privacy challenges specific to healthcare big data analytics. For each, describe one technical mitigation (e.g., differential privacy, federated learning, de-identification).
  1. Heterogeneous formats/standards across hospitals (HL7, FHIR, DICOM, free-text notes, different ICD versions). Fix: map to a common model (FHIR / OMOP CDM) in an ETL layer.
  2. Data cannot legally leave the hospital (HIPAA/GDPR). Fix: use federated learning, which shares model weights instead of records.
  3. Re-identification risk in released aggregates or datasets (rare diagnoses, DOB + region). Fix: Safe Harbour de-identification (remove 18 identifiers) plus differential privacy (Laplace noise on aggregate queries).

Pool · v1 deck · DiscussionQ10.14 Kwak et al. found Twitter's average shortest path is 4.12 hops over 41.7M users. If a misinformation tweet is retweeted by a high-PageRank account, estimate how many users it reaches within 3 hops assuming an average out-degree of 45. What graph property makes this estimate an overestimate?

Reach within h hops ≈ d + d2 + d3 (tree assumption)

  1. Hop 1: 45
  2. Hop 2: 452 = 2,025
  3. Hop 3: 453 = 91,125
  4. Total ≈ 45 + 2,025 + 91,125 = 93,195 users

Meaning: about 93 K users (0.2% of 41.7 M) in 3 hops. This is an overestimate because of clustering / triadic closure (high clustering coefficient, overlapping follower sets, reciprocity). Many hop-2/3 users are the same people counted again. Also, not every follower retweets.

Pool · v1 deck · DiscussionQ10.15 A Prophet model for weekly hospital admissions has strong annual and weekly seasonality but misses a sudden COVID-19 wave onset. Which Prophet component should detect this changepoint, and what prior hyperparameter controls the model's sensitivity to new changepoints?

The trend component g(t) (piecewise-linear/logistic trend with changepoints) should capture the onset. changepoint_prior_scale controls sensitivity (default 0.05; it is the scale of the Laplace prior on rate changes). Raising it (e.g. 0.5) makes the trend react more to the wave, but risks overfitting noise. Also relevant: n_changepoints and changepoint_range (default 0.8, so Prophet places no changepoints in the last 20% of history unless you raise it).

Pool · ProgrammingQ10.17 Live Exercise (Part 2): Prophet flu forecasting. (1) Generate 3 years of synthetic weekly ILI data with annual seasonality + noise; (2) fit Prophet with yearly and weekly seasonality; (3) plot a 12-week forecast with 95% uncertainty intervals; (4) add an "Eid" holiday (spike in healthcare visits) and observe the effect; (5) change changepoint_prior_scale from 0.05 to 0.5 and compare trend flexibility.
dates = pd.date_range("2021-01-04", periods=156, freq="W")
ili = (5 + 3*np.sin(2*np.pi*np.arange(156)/52 - np.pi/2)
       + rng.normal(0, 0.5, 156)).clip(0)
df = pd.DataFrame({"ds": dates, "y": ili})
hol = pd.DataFrame({"holiday": "Eid", "ds": pd.to_datetime(
    ["2021-05-13", "2022-05-03", "2023-04-22"]),
    "lower_window": 0, "upper_window": 3})
for cps in (0.05, 0.5):
    m = Prophet(yearly_seasonality=True, weekly_seasonality=True,
                holidays=hol, changepoint_prior_scale=cps,
                interval_width=0.95).fit(df)
    fc = m.predict(m.make_future_dataframe(periods=12, freq="W"))
    m.plot(fc); m.plot_components(fc)

3 years = 156 weeks, 52-week period. With weekly data, weekly seasonality is not identifiable (one point per week). A larger changepoint_prior_scale gives a wigglier trend that starts to follow noise.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.18 What are the four structural properties of social network graphs described in this chapter? For each, state one implication for how information spreads.
  1. Sparsity: edges ≪ n2. Spread follows few paths, so seeding matters.
  2. Power-law (scale-free) degree distribution: a few hubs exist. Content that reaches a hub spreads explosively, so hubs are the targets for campaigns or blocking.
  3. Small-world / short paths (Twitter avg 4.12 hops): information can reach most of the network within a few hops.
  4. High clustering / community structure (with low reciprocity on Twitter, 22%): content circulates inside communities (echo chambers). It needs bridge nodes to cross between them.
Book ch10 · ShortQ10.19 Describe the two phases of the Louvain community detection algorithm. What is the modularity objective Q, and why does maximising it detect communities?

Phase 1 (local moving): move each node to the neighbouring community that gives the largest modularity gain ΔQ. Repeat until no gain. Phase 2 (aggregation): turn each community into a super-node (sum edge weights) and repeat phase 1 until Q stops rising.

Q = (1/2m) Σij [Aij − kikj/2m] δ(ci, cj) is the fraction of edges inside communities minus the fraction expected in a random graph with the same degrees. Maximising it finds groups that are more densely connected internally than chance.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.20 Why is PageRank a better measure of user influence on Twitter than raw follower count? Describe a scenario where the two measures rank users differently.

PageRank weights a follower by that follower's own importance. It measures who listens, not just how many. Bots, inactive accounts and follow-back accounts inflate follower counts. Kwak et al. found the rankings differ, and retweet influence differs again. Scenario: account A has 1 M followers, mostly bots. Account B has 20 K followers, including major journalists and news outlets. A ranks higher by followers. B ranks higher by PageRank.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.21 VADER outputs a compound score for social media text. For each tweet say whether the compound score is higher or lower than the plain form and which VADER rule causes it: (a) "GREAT concert" vs "great concert"; (b) "not good" vs "good"; (c) "absolutely fantastic!!!" vs "fantastic".

(a) Higher: the capitalisation rule. An ALL-CAPS sentiment word in mixed-case text is intensified.

(b) Lower (turns negative): the negation rule. "not" within 3 words flips polarity (scaled by about −0.74).

(c) Higher: the degree modifier (booster) "absolutely" plus exclamation-mark emphasis (up to 3 counted).

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.22 What is autocorrelation in a time series, and why does it invalidate standard regression's standard errors? Give a business example where strong autocorrelation is expected.

Autocorrelation is the correlation of a series with its own lagged values (yt with yt−k). OLS assumes independent errors. With positive autocorrelation, each observation carries less new information. Standard errors are then underestimated, and t-tests/p-values look falsely significant. Example: daily e-commerce transactions. Today's volume tracks yesterday's and the same weekday last week (lag-1 and lag-7).

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.23 Describe each component of Prophet's model y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + ε(t). What hyperparameter controls trend flexibility, and what happens if it is set too high?
  • g(t) trend: piecewise-linear or logistic (with capacity) growth with changepoints.
  • s(t) seasonality: Fourier series for yearly/weekly/daily cycles.
  • h(t) holidays/events: user-supplied dates with windows.
  • ε(t) error: noise the model does not explain.

changepoint_prior_scale (default 0.05) controls trend flexibility. If it is too high, the trend overfits by chasing noise and outliers. The forecast uncertainty intervals then become very wide.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.24 A Prophet model for weekly hospital outpatient visits shows an unexplained spike every year in late April and late June. Which component is responsible? What information would you add to capture them explicitly?

The yearly seasonality s(t) absorbs the recurring spikes, or they stay in the residuals ε. They tie to events, so add a holidays dataframe h(t) with those dates (e.g. Eid, which moves on the Gregorian calendar, Pohela Boishakh, school terms, vaccination campaigns). Give them lower/upper windows so Prophet models them explicitly.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.25 What are STL decomposition and Isolation Forest? For each, describe what type of time-series anomaly it is best suited to detect.

STL (Seasonal-Trend decomposition using LOESS) splits a series into trend + seasonal + remainder. Flag large remainders. It works best for contextual/seasonal anomalies: values unusual for that time of day/week (e.g. normal traffic at 3 a.m.).

Isolation Forest isolates points with random splits. Anomalies need fewer splits (short path length). It works best for point/global anomalies in feature space (sudden spikes, multivariate outliers). It does not model seasonality.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.26 What is the difference between safe harbour de-identification and differential privacy for healthcare data? Give one scenario where safe harbour is sufficient and one where differential privacy would also be needed.

Safe harbour (HIPAA) removes the 18 listed identifiers (names, IDs, dates except year, small geographic units, etc.) from records. It is rule-based and applies to the dataset. Differential privacy is a mathematical guarantee (ε). Adding calibrated noise (e.g. Laplace) makes outputs nearly unchanged whether or not any one person is in the data.

Safe harbour is enough for an internal research team under a data-use agreement that analyses de-identified readmission records. You also need DP when publishing aggregate statistics or an open query interface (e.g. rare-disease counts by small region). There, linkage or repeated queries could re-identify patients.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.27 What was the core argument of Obermeyer & Emanuel (2016)? List two clinical tasks where they argued ML outperforms physician heuristics, and the metric used.

Argument: big data and ML will transform medicine. Models built on large EHR data predict better than clinicians' rules of thumb. So ML will reshape prognosis, displace work in radiology and anatomic pathology, and improve diagnostic accuracy.

Tasks: (1) prognosis / mortality prediction (e.g. death in end-of-life care, cancer outcomes). (2) Image-based diagnosis in radiology/pathology. Metric: AUROC (area under ROC curve / C-statistic).

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.28 Explain why Google Flu Trends over-predicted flu incidence in 2012–2013. Name all three causes identified by Lazer et al. (2014) and say which is most fundamental.

GFT predicted more than double the CDC's ILI level. Lazer et al. give three causes. (1) Big data hubris: treating search data as a substitute for, not a supplement to, traditional surveillance. GFT over-fit ~50 M terms to ~1,000 data points. It picked terms that matched winter seasonality, not flu. (2) Algorithm dynamics: Google changed search (autocomplete, suggested health terms). That shifted query behaviour in ways the model could not see. (3) Lack of transparency / no recalibration with CDC data (closed, unreplicable methods).

The most fundamental cause is big data hubris: correlation without causal validation on a non-stationary data-generating process.

Book ch10 · ShortQ10.29 What is federated learning? Why is it used in multi-hospital clinical ML, and what is the primary limitation of the federated averaging algorithm?

Federated learning trains a shared model by sending the model to the data. Each hospital trains locally. Only weights/gradients go to a central server, which averages them. Hospitals use it because HIPAA/GDPR forbid pooling patient records. Main limitation of FedAvg: non-IID data across hospitals (different populations, coding) makes the averaged model converge slowly or perform poorly for some sites. Other limits are high communication cost and gradient leakage risk.

Book ch10 · AnalyticalQ10.30 Influence Maximisation Calculation. A Facebook group for a dengue alert campaign has 1 million users; power-law degree distribution, α = 2.3; budget k = 5 seed posts. (a) Under the independent cascade model with p_uv = 0.05, will seeding the 5 highest-follower users or the 5 highest-PageRank users spread further? Use Kwak et al. (b) Greedy (Kempe et al.) guarantees (1 − 1/e) ≈ 0.632 of optimum. If σ(S*) = 200,000, what is the worst-case greedy influence? Is this meaningful for a public health campaign? (c) σ(S) is estimated by 10,000 Monte Carlo cascades; with 1M nodes, k = 5, 100 candidates per greedy step, how many cascade simulations are needed? Tractable on one machine?

(a) PageRank seeds are likely better. Kwak et al. showed follower count, PageRank and retweet influence rank users differently. High-follower accounts often have inactive/bot followers and overlapping audiences. PageRank rewards being followed by well-connected users, so cascades with p = 0.05 travel further. Top-follower hubs in a power-law graph also overlap, which gives redundant reach. Best: choose seeds in different communities.

(b) σ(Sgreedy) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·σ(S*)

  1. 1 − 1/e = 0.6321
  2. 0.6321 × 200,000 = 126,424 users

Meaning: in the worst case, greedy reaches ~126 K of 1 M users. This works as a floor, and in practice greedy is usually close to optimal. But it is relative to σ(S*), which depends on the assumed puv. If p is wrong, the guarantee says nothing about real reach.

(c) Simulations = k × candidates × MC runs

  1. 5 × 100 × 10,000 = 5,000,000 cascades
  2. At ~0.1 s per cascade on a 1 M-node graph (assumed): 5 × 106 × 0.1 = 500,000 s ≈ 139 h ≈ 5.8 days

Meaning: this is barely tractable on one machine. Use CELF lazy evaluation (up to ~700× fewer evaluations), parallelise simulations on Spark, or use sketch-based methods (RIS).

Book ch10 · AnalyticalQ10.31 Prophet Model Diagnostics.
